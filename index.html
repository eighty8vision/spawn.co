Side quest

The hardest calendar problem I’ve untangled

The calendar was a symptom.

In the early days, I reported directly to the CEO of the company that was later acquired by NOBULL. The business was growing at such a rapid rate that the CEO was spread too thin and always trying to play catch-up. I recognized that pattern really quickly, and I also recognized the negative impact it was having on the business and on her. I quickly understood the ways in which she needed to be communicated with, where the business needed her, and where she was in her own way.

The deeper problem was resource and capacity allocation, which is a constant juggling act in a growing company. It’s a symptom of growth. When capacity has been hit, people, process, and systems need to continually evolve to supply more capacity.

I got to the source and started solving from there. I started managing the team more heavily, getting as much upfront information and as many decision-making options as possible, all filtering through me. That made it so she had to spend as little time thinking and processing as possible, because I had done all of that for her. Everything that reached her was either informing her, or a very clear decision with a very clear understanding of the options, outcomes, and consequences she could choose from.

That not only created relief on her calendar, because she simply needed to be in fewer places, but it developed a cadence as we grew. We recognized when capacity was hit and we were all reaching a point of diminishing returns, and we solved the capacity problem, whether it was in people, process, or systems. This mindset is what enables unlimited growth. I remained the funnel and the filter for as many things as I possibly could, and trained the organization how to function and interact with her.
